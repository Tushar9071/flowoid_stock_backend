Executive Map

This system is a tenant-based business management backend. It does not treat stock as a single manually edited number. Finished inventory is the result of manufacturing activity, packaging, adjustments, and dispatch. Dealer outstanding is the result of opening balances, sale ledger entries created during dispatch, and payment ledger entries.

Business Rule

Sales depend on packaged stock

An order can be drafted for active dealer and active designs, but it cannot be confirmed unless packaged dozens are available after considering other confirmed/packed/partially dispatched orders.

Manufacturing Rule

Prepared stock starts with worker returns

Raw material is issued only through worker assignments. Accepted returned pieces become unpackaged prepared stock; rejected pieces increase rejection tracking but do not become saleable stock.

Finance Rule

Ledger is event-driven

Opening balance creates a ledger entry. Dispatch creates SALE ledger entries. Dealer receipts, advances, supplier payments, cancellations, and bounced payments create payment or reversal ledger entries.

Document Rule

PDF and WhatsApp follow operations

Invoices and challans are generated only for dispatched or partially dispatched orders. WhatsApp reuses or generates documents, uploads the PDF to Meta, sends a template, and records message status logs.

High-Level Architecture

The backend is an Express API with tenant-scoped routes. Controllers validate request data and call service functions. Prisma models represent business entities and service functions enforce the operational rules.

Main Data Boundaries

Tenant isolates business data. Most business routes are mounted under /api/tenants/:tenantId. Users access tenants through TenantUser, except SUPER_ADMIN.

Party is shared by supplier and dealer workflows. The type field decides whether the same entity can be used in purchases, orders, or payments.

Design is the SKU-like master for manufacturing, inventory, and sales.

Most Important Mental Model

There are two stock layers for a design: unpackaged pieces and packaged dozens. Workers return pieces. Packaging consumes 12 pieces per dozen. Sales dispatch consumes packaged dozens.

Lifecycle Of Stock And Items

The code has three different inventory concepts. Raw material stock is material-level. Prepared stock is design-level. Saleable stock is packaged dozens only.

stateDiagram-v2
  [*] --> RawMaterialType: create material type
  RawMaterialType --> RawPurchasePending: create purchase PENDING
  RawPurchasePending --> RawPurchaseReceived: status RECEIVED
  RawMaterialType --> RawPurchaseReceived: create purchase RECEIVED
  RawPurchaseReceived --> RawAvailable: contributes to raw stock
  RawAvailable --> AssignmentIssued: create worker assignment
  AssignmentIssued --> InProgress: mark status IN_PROGRESS
  AssignmentIssued --> PartialReturn: record accepted goods return
  InProgress --> PartialReturn: record accepted goods return
  PartialReturn --> Completed: returnedPieces >= expectedPieces
  PartialReturn --> PartialReturn: additional partial return
  Completed --> UnpackagedPreparedPieces: accepted pieces are stock input
  UnpackagedPreparedPieces --> PackagedDozens: packaging batch, 12 pieces per dozen
  PackagedDozens --> Reserved: order confirm validates/reserves logically
  Reserved --> Packed: mark order packed
  Packed --> Dispatched: dispatch consumes packaged dozens
  Dispatched --> [*]

Raw Material Formula

current raw stock = RECEIVED raw purchases - raw material issuances

Manual raw material issuance is explicitly disabled. The backend tells users to create issuances through assignment flow.

Unpackaged Prepared Formula

unpackaged pieces = accepted goods returns + unpackaged adjustments - pieces used in packaging

Rejected worker pieces do not enter stock. They stay on assignment/return records for quality and worker accounting.

Packaged Stock Formula

packaged dozens = packaging batches + packaged adjustments - dispatched dozens

Order confirmation and dispatch both use synced packaged stock to prevent negative inventory.

Flow Of Money, Ledger, And Cash

There is no separate general ledger engine; the party ledger is the financial backbone. The same ledger table tracks opening balances, sale debits, payment credits/debits, and reversals.

sequenceDiagram
  participant Owner as Owner/Cashier
  participant Orders as Orders Service
  participant Ledger as PartyLedgerEntry
  participant Payments as Payments Service
  participant Reports as Outstanding/Cashflow/Aging

  Owner->>Orders: Dispatch dealer order
  Orders->>Ledger: Create SALE debit for dispatched amount
  Orders->>Ledger: Recalculate running balances
  Owner->>Payments: Record dealer payment
  Payments->>Payments: Validate dealer and allocations
  Payments->>Ledger: Create PAYMENT_RECEIVED credit
  Payments->>Ledger: Recalculate running balances
  Reports->>Ledger: Outstanding = debits - credits
  Reports->>Payments: Cashflow = cleared receipts - cleared supplier payments
  Reports->>Payments: Aging = unpaid dispatched orders by due date
Dealer sale

Dispatch writes a SALE ledger row. For partial dispatch, the sale amount is proportional to dispatched subtotal. Final dispatch writes any remaining amount so the ledger total equals order total after discount.

Dealer payment

A dealer payment with allocations becomes DEALER_RECEIPT. Without allocations it becomes DEALER_ADVANCE. Both create PAYMENT_RECEIVED credit entries.

Supplier payment

Supplier payments create SUPPLIER_PAYMENT records and ledger entries as money paid out. Raw material purchases themselves do not currently create purchase ledger entries.

Payment reversal

When a payment is marked BOUNCED or CANCELLED, the service creates a reversal ledger row instead of deleting historical ledger impact.

Business Scenario Flows

These are practical operating stories, written in the order a real jewelry business should use the system.

Scenario 1: Raw Material Purchase -> Stock Preparation -> Sale -> Payment

flowchart LR
  A["Create supplier"] --> B["Create raw material type"]
  B --> C["Record RECEIVED raw purchase"]
  C --> D["Create design + supplementary needs"]
  D --> E["Create worker assignment"]
  E --> F["Raw material issuance created automatically"]
  E --> G["Supplementary stock decremented"]
  F --> H["Record goods return"]
  H --> I["Accepted pieces become unpackaged stock"]
  I --> J["Package pieces into dozens"]
  J --> K["Create dealer order"]
  K --> L["Confirm against packaged stock"]
  L --> M["Pack order"]
  M --> N["Dispatch"]
  N --> O["SALE ledger debit"]
  O --> P["Generate invoice/challan"]
  P --> Q["Record payment"]
  Q --> R["PAYMENT_RECEIVED ledger credit"]
  1. Set up the supplier under Parties with type SUPPLIER.
  2. Create the raw material type, then create a raw material purchase. Only purchases with status RECEIVED count as stock.
  3. Create design category and design. The design stores worker piece rate and sale price per dozen.
  4. Optionally define supplementary needs on the design. These act as a template: assignment quantity is quantityPerPiece * expectedPieces.
  5. Create a worker assignment. The backend checks active worker, active design, active raw material, raw stock availability, and supplementary availability. It automatically creates raw material issuance and supplementary issuances.
  6. Record goods returns when the worker brings items back. Accepted pieces increase assignment returnedPieces, worker totalEarned, and prepared stock calculation.
  7. Package finished pieces. The backend requires enough unpackaged pieces and consumes exactly 12 pieces for each dozen.
  8. Create dealer order. The order starts as DRAFT, so it can be edited.
  9. Confirm order. The backend validates packaged stock after subtracting other active reservations.
  10. Pack and dispatch. Dispatch creates dispatch items, increments each order item dispatched quantity, syncs inventory, updates order status, and creates SALE ledger entries.
  11. Generate invoice/challan, optionally send through WhatsApp, then record dealer payment. Payment creates payment allocation and payment ledger entries.

Scenario 2: Dealer Wants Item X But Prepared Stock Does Not Exist

flowchart TD
  A["Dealer asks for Item X"] --> B{"Design X exists and active?"}
  B -- No --> B1["Create/activate design with sale price and piece rate"]
  B -- Yes --> C{"Packaged dozens available?"}
  B1 --> C
  C -- Yes --> O["Create order -> confirm -> pack -> dispatch"]
  C -- No --> D{"Unpackaged pieces available?"}
  D -- Yes --> P["Create packaging batch"]
  P --> O
  D -- No --> E{"Raw material available?"}
  E -- No --> R["Purchase/receive raw material first"]
  R --> F
  E -- Yes --> F["Assign worker to make Item X"]
  F --> G["Worker returns goods"]
  G --> H["Accepted pieces sync into unpackaged stock"]
  H --> P

Why the sale cannot proceed: confirmation requires packaged dozens. The system intentionally blocks confirmation when packaged stock is insufficient, because dispatch would otherwise create negative stock and incorrect dealer ledger.

Correct operating path: check inventory stock for the design. If unpackaged stock exists, package it. If not, create a worker assignment. If raw stock is short, receive raw material first. Only after packaging should the order be confirmed.

What changes internally: assignment consumes raw/supplementary material, goods return creates accepted pieces, packaging creates saleable dozens, confirmation reserves stock logically, dispatch reduces packaged stock and creates the SALE ledger entry.

Scenario 4: Partial Payments And Cash Flow

  1. Dealer order is dispatched. This creates dealer receivable in the party ledger.
  2. Dealer pays part of the amount. Frontend records POST /payments/dealer with one or more allocations.
  3. The payment amount may be split across multiple orders. The backend rejects allocations to draft or cancelled orders and rejects orders belonging to another dealer.
  4. The payment creates a PAYMENT_RECEIVED ledger credit. Outstanding becomes ledger debits minus credits.
  5. Cashflow reads cleared payments for a day and separates dealer receipts from supplier payments.
  6. If cheque/transfer fails later, mark payment BOUNCED. The backend creates reversal ledger entry and cash/outstanding reports stop treating it as valid.

Critical Frontend Validations

Order confirm
Pre-check GET /inventory/stock/:designId. Still call confirm and display backend shortage details, because reservations by other orders can change.
Assignment creation
Show raw stock and supplementary requirements before submit. Warn when expected pieces multiplied by supplementary needs exceeds stock.
Payment allocation
Prevent allocation total above payment amount. Do not allocate to draft/cancelled orders or another dealer's order.
Packaging
Show unpackaged pieces and calculate required pieces as dozens * 12.
Documents
Disable invoice/challan before partial/full dispatch. Label challan as latest dispatch-based but includes all dispatch items in generated document.
WhatsApp
Normalize Indian phone numbers visually; backend accepts 10-digit or 12-digit numeric forms and converts to E.164-like 91....

Common Mistakes To Avoid

  • Do not show raw material issuance as a normal manual operation. The backend returns a validation error and tells users to use assignment flow.
  • Do not treat InventoryStock as the only source of truth. The service recalculates it from activity tables.
  • Do not create payments as a substitute for dispatch. Dealer outstanding from sales appears only after SALE ledger entries are written during dispatch.
  • Do not assume an order is financially due when it is only DRAFT or CONFIRMED. Aging reports use partially/full dispatched orders.
  • Do not allow design edits that silently affect existing worker assignments. The assignment locks pieceRateAtAssignment, so historical earning stays stable.
  • Do not hide rejection fields in goods return. Rejected pieces affect worker quality tracking and explain why expected pieces may not become saleable stock.

Business Owner Guide

Use the system in the same order the business actually works. Think of it as four counters: material counter, worker counter, stock counter, and sales/cash counter.

1. Set Up Masters

Add dealers, suppliers, workers, raw materials, supplementary materials, and designs. A design is the item you sell. It carries the worker rate and sale price.

2. Buy Material

Record raw material purchases when material arrives. If material is only ordered but not received, keep it pending so it does not appear as usable stock.

3. Give Work To Worker

Create an assignment when you give material to a worker. The system reduces raw material stock and supplementary stock immediately, because those goods are now with the worker.

4. Receive Finished Goods

When the worker returns goods, enter how many pieces came back and how many were rejected. Only accepted pieces become stock.

5. Pack Before Selling

The system sells in dozens. Loose accepted pieces must be packed into dozens before orders can be confirmed.

6. Sell And Collect

Create dealer order, confirm stock, pack, dispatch, generate bill, send by WhatsApp, then record payment. Outstanding reports update from these entries.

Common Operational Mistakes

Selling before packaging: The system blocks this because loose pieces are not saleable dozens yet.

Skipping worker return: If worker returns are not entered, prepared stock will not increase.

Entering payment before dispatch: This can be recorded as advance, but it will not create a sale. The sale happens only when goods are dispatched.

Using wrong party type: Suppliers are for material purchases and supplier payments. Dealers are for sales orders and dealer receipts.
